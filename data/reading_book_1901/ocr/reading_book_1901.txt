EXPLANATORY NOTE.

Tue hope that it would be useful to the missionary
philologically, as well as for practical school work
in our stations, suggested to me the production of
some such little book as this.

It seemed probable that a book wholly native,
both in matter and diction, would contain more of
the idioms and peculiarities of the language than
could be found in a translation, and also that the
European element in it would be almost nil.

These selections of Gogo Folk-lore, etc., may also
prove more attractive, as a reading book, to the
native scholar than pages more pretentious but
confined to subjects altogether foreign to him,
and in which European influence necessarily manifests
itself in the language.

If the hope expressed above be realized, we shall
owe a debt of gratitude to our native teachers at
Mpwapwa, Andereya and Nhonya, who, without
assistance or supervision, have collected and written
out the whole of this—the first all-native book in
Gogo.

The only part I have taken in the work has been
to decipher the original manuscript (not always an
easy proceeding), to re-write the whole, punctuate,
and see through the press.

J.-E. B.
December 1900.
